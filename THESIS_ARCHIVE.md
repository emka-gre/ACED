# Thesis archive

The research this application is built on lives in a bachelor thesis repository that is
**not** part of this repo. It is 1.1 GB, most of it a committed virtualenv and intermediate
datasets, and it is the historical record of the research rather than a runtime dependency.

## Where it is

```
~/Desktop/aced-thesis-archive/
├── bachelor-project-main.zip     # the archive as received, unmodified (1.1 GB)
└── extracted/bachelor-project-main/
    ├── training/                 # models, scaler, LSTM training notebook
    ├── ML_pipeline/              # the train/val/test splits the models were fitted on
    ├── streamlit/                # the three research pipeline modules
    ├── features/features.py      # the feature computations being ported
    ├── dataset/                  # windowing, dataset construction, utilities
    ├── labeling/csv_pose/        # raw per-video MediaPipe pose CSVs
    └── video_62_B_Ema.MOV        # the only video in the archive
```

Only a subset is extracted; anything else can be pulled from the zip on demand.


The filenames differ from the archive's: `df_X_m_train_3split.csv` here against
`ML_pipeline/df_X_m_train_3split_2.csv` there. **They are the same data**, established the
same way the scaler provenance was: refitting a `StandardScaler` on this file reproduces the
shipped LSTM scaler's `mean_` and `scale_` with a maximum absolute difference of `0.0`, and
its 1588 rows match the scaler's `n_samples_seen_`. A file that were merely similar could not
do that.

That correspondence is what makes `df_X_m_val_3split.csv` usable as the held-out set for task
3.6: it is the validation half of the same split the shipped model was fitted on. It is
balanced — 89 fault windows, 89 sound.

`scripts/evaluate_bundles.py` reads these. Copy them to `.thesis-data/` in the repo (which is
gitignored, as every CSV outside `fixtures/` is) or pass `--data` with the directory.

## What is deliberately left behind

The committed virtualenv (50,667 of the archive's 51,311 files), ~400 MB of intermediate
CSVs, the YOLO experiment branch, the labeling tools, the Streamlit prototype, the
training notebooks, and the thesis writing. None of it is needed to run the application.

## Verified facts about the artifacts

Established by inspecting the archive directly, and relied on by the change proposal:

- `training/LTSM/scaler.pkl` is the scaler fitted during LSTM training. Refitting a
  `StandardScaler` on `ML_pipeline/df_X_m_train_3split_2.csv` (1588×480, after dropping the
  unnamed index column) reproduces its `mean_` and `scale_` with a maximum absolute
  difference of `0.0`, and `training/LTSM/ltsm_training.ipynb` fits on exactly that file.
  Replacing the research pipeline's inference-time refit with a load of this scaler
  therefore changes no prediction.
- `training/MODELS/SVM_model` scales its own input. It must be fed **raw** features;
  applying a bundle-level scaler to it would double-scale silently.
- The archive contains **no recorded pipeline output**. Parity references have to be
  generated by running the research pipeline once under Python 3.11.
- Two SVM artifacts exist — `training/MODELS/SVM_model` (1.9 MB) and
  `training/SVM/SVM_model` (2.5 MB). Confirm which the thesis reports on before building
  the SVM bundle.
